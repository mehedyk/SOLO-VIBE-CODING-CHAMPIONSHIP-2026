Inside the lab that's teaching oceans to remember

The first thing you notice about the lab is that it doesn't look like one. It is a converted sardine cannery, and it smells faintly of the fact. The second thing you notice is the wall: three hundred and forty small screens, each one a buoy, each one blinking at a slightly different rate.

"They're not measuring the sea," says Dr Rui Ferreira, who runs the place on a budget he describes as "optimistic". "They're learning what it usually does, so they can tell us the moment it stops doing that."

"A thermometer forgets. We wanted something that doesn't."Dr Rui Ferreira, Instituto do Mar

What the buoys have found, over five years, is a pattern nobody had a model for — a slow, seasonal shift in the way warm water stacks on cold that seems to predict Atlantic storm tracks roughly eleven days earlier than anything currently in use.
